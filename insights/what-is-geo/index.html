GEO vs. SEO: What Changes for Indian Regional Brands

Digital & GEO 7 min read Mamta Advertising
On This Page
Three Search Tracks What Is GEO GEO vs. SEO The Regional-Brand Context What to Do Now

Until recently, "getting found online" meant one thing: ranking on Google. That's still important — but it's no longer the whole picture, and it matters even more for regional and Gujarati-language brands than it does for national ones, because the gap between "well optimised for Google" and "visible to an AI answer engine" is currently wider for regional content than for English-language national content.

Search has split into three parallel tracks

Track one: traditional search — someone types a query into Google and gets blue links back. Track two: AI-assisted search — someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity a question and gets a synthesised answer that may or may not mention your brand. Track three: social search — recommendations discovered on Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, or WhatsApp forwards. Most regional brands and their agencies build almost exclusively for Track One, and nothing at all for Track Two.

GEO, defined simply

Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO) is the practice of structuring your content and digital presence so AI answer engines are more likely to surface, cite, and recommend your brand when someone asks a related question. AI models draw on authoritative, well-structured, frequently cited sources when generating an answer — if your business isn't one of those sources, you simply don't exist in that conversation, no matter how well you rank on page one of Google.

Your competitor isn't just outranking you on Google. They may already be the answer ChatGPT gives when someone in your own city asks about your category.

GEO vs. SEO — not a replacement, a parallel track

SEO optimises for crawlability, backlinks, keyword relevance, and page speed. GEO optimises for citation quality, content authority, and being the source an AI model trusts enough to quote. A brand can have strong SEO and near-zero GEO visibility at the same time — and for many Gujarati and regional-language businesses, that's exactly the current situation. The two are complementary, not competing; the smartest approach treats them as one connected discipline, not two separate budgets.

What changes specifically for Indian regional brands

Three things are different for a Gujarati-language or regional brand compared to a national English-language one, and each needs its own fix:

  • Training-data gap. AI models have historically seen far less well-structured Gujarati and regional-language business content than English content, which means regional brands have more ground to make up — but also a genuine first-mover advantage if they act now.
  • Bilingual content strategy. Publishing the same authoritative content in both English and the regional language, structured the same way, increases the chance of being cited regardless of which language the query was asked in.
  • Local-business signal consistency. AI engines lean heavily on structured, consistent business information — name, address, phone, and service description matching exactly across your website, Google Business Profile, and directory listings. Inconsistency here hurts GEO visibility more than it hurts traditional SEO.

What regional brands should do right now

First: audit your AI visibility — ask ChatGPT and Gemini who the leading businesses are in your category, in your city. Are you mentioned? Second: identify the specific questions your customers are already asking AI assistants that you should be answering — "best [category] in Ahmedabad" style queries. Third: build genuinely useful, well-structured content in both English and your regional language that directly and authoritatively answers those questions, rather than generic promotional copy an AI model has no reason to trust or cite.
